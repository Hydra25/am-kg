# DEMO / SYNTHETIC: Energy density, keyhole porosity and hardness of selective laser melted AlSi10Mg

## Abstract
DEMO / SYNTHETIC TEXT generated to test the AM-KG pipeline; the numbers are not real research results. AlSi10Mg samples were produced by selective laser melting (SLM) using a laser power of 0.35 kW and scan speeds between 1000 and 1600 mm/s. A volumetric energy density above 70 J/mm³ caused keyhole porosity. T6 heat treatment reduced the hardness of the alloy.

## 1. Introduction
Aluminium alloys processed by SLM show fine cellular microstructures.

## 2. Experimental procedure
AlSi10Mg powder was processed on an SLM machine with a layer thickness of 30 µm and a hatch spacing of 130 μm. The volumetric energy density ranged from 40 to 90 J/mm3. The build plate temperature was 150 °C. Relative density was measured with the Archimedes method, and the microstructure was characterized by optical microscopy and SEM. Hardness was measured using a Vickers hardness tester.

## 3. Results
The highest relative density of 99.4 % was obtained at an energy density of 60 J/mm³. Increasing the scan speed reduced the volumetric energy density and increased lack of fusion. Energy densities above 70 J/mm³ promoted keyhole porosity. The as-built hardness was 125 HV0.1. The as-built microstructure showed a cellular Si network along melt pool boundaries.

## 4. Discussion
The T6 heat treatment dissolved the cellular Si network and decreased the hardness to 95 HV. The laser power did not affect the surface roughness in the investigated range.

## 5. Conclusion
Volumetric energy density influenced keyhole porosity and lack of fusion in SLM AlSi10Mg.
